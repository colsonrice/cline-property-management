What a mowing visit includes

Every visit is the full job, not just the open turf. We cut at the right height for the season, alternate direction to keep the grass standing, string-trim everything the deck can't reach, edge the hard surfaces, and blow the clippings off drives, walks and patios.

  • Mow at seasonally-adjusted height, direction alternated each visit
  • String trimming around beds, posts, trees and foundations
  • Mechanical edging along drives, walks and curb lines
  • Full blow-off of all hard surfaces before we leave
  • Clippings dispersed or removed depending on the property

Properties we mow

The equipment and the routing change a lot depending on what we're cutting, so it's worth being specific about it.

Residential

Weekly or bi-weekly cuts on a fixed day. Gated yards, tight side yards and detail-heavy landscaping are no problem.

Commercial

Retail, office and industrial frontage kept presentable on a schedule that works around your business hours.

HOA & Common Areas

Entrances, monument signs, common turf and detention areas — the shared spaces that set the tone for a whole neighborhood.

Municipal

Right-of-way, median and roadside mowing, including the wide, rough and steep ground that needs the bigger equipment.

Rough cutting and overgrown lots

Not every job is a manicured lawn. Vacant lots, easements, detention basins and properties that have gotten away from someone need brush-capable equipment and a different approach. We take those on as one-time reclaims or on a seasonal rotation.

FAQ

Common questions

Which towns do you mow?

Our mowing routes cover Whitestown, Zionsville and West Carmel. We keep mowing tight to those three so we can hold a consistent day-of-week schedule. Our other services — mulching, cleanups, leaf removal, snow and washing — travel further out, so it's always worth asking.

Weekly or every other week?

Weekly through the main growing season is what we recommend and what most properties end up on. Bi-weekly works in the shoulder months or on slower-growing turf, but during May and June a two-week gap usually means cutting off more than a third of the blade, which stresses the lawn.

Do I need to be home?

No. As long as we can get to the turf — gates unlocked, pets in — we'll take care of it and you'll come home to it finished.

What happens if it rains on my day?

We shift to the next workable day rather than cutting a saturated lawn, which rutted turf and clumped clippings make worse. If the weather pushes us more than a day or two, you'll hear from us.
